# Sepia Atlas — Episode 1 research plan

Working title: **What Life Really Looked Like in America in 1900**

Status: research plan and shot-list skeleton. The narration will be written only after the
source review, and no image is approved until its rights are checked. This episode stays private
until the owner approves it.

## Editorial angle

Not one America, but several coexisting ones. A streetcar city, a company town, a farm without
electricity, an immigrant neighborhood and a factory floor all belonged to the same year. The
episode follows one ordinary day across those worlds: waking, getting to work, school, shopping,
lighting the evening. It ends on how quickly the 1900 world disappeared.

## Structure

| Chapter | Narration beat | Visual subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | A single street photograph, and a detail that dates it exactly | One dense 1900 street scene |
| Morning | Housing, water, heating, lighting | Tenement kitchens, farmhouse interiors |
| Getting there | Streetcars, horses, walking distances | Tracks, depots, crowded crossings |
| Work | Factory, mill, office, farm labor | Work floors, child labor records |
| School | Public school, rural schoolhouse, access | Classrooms and schoolyards |
| Buying | General store, department store, catalog | Storefronts and shop interiors |
| After hours | Parks, saloons, theaters, home | Leisure scenes at dusk |
| Turning point | Electricity, telephones, automobiles arriving | The new machines in the old streets |
| Consequences | Who gained, who was left out | Contrast pairs, same city, different lives |
| Ending | The last photograph of a street that no longer exists | One quiet vanished place |

## Candidate archives

- Library of Congress, Detroit Publishing Company Collection, c. 1880–1920.
- Library of Congress, "America at Work, America at Leisure" motion pictures, 1894–1915.
- Library of Congress, Farm Security Administration/Office of War Information (later period,
  only where the narration explicitly marks the difference).
- US National Archives, Record Group 29 (census) and RG 83 (agriculture photographs).
- Smithsonian Open Access, photography collections.
- Wikimedia Commons, only with an explicit public-domain or compatible license and attribution.
- State historical societies and university digital libraries for vanished towns.

## Image record fields

Every selected image must record: internal ID, file, original title, institution, author, date or
period, place, source page URL, download URL, license or documented permission, attribution
requirement, narration segment, in/out time and historical verification result.

## Rights rules

- "Available online" is not "free to reuse".
- "No known restrictions" is not automatically public domain.
- Any unclear license is excluded until the owner approves it.
- Generated or illustrative images never stand in for a photograph of a real event, and any
  diagram is labelled as an original explanatory graphic.

## Production limits

The current renderer produces a still background under the narration. The archival montage needs
the documentary render step (Ken Burns on a timed image list) before this episode can be final.
The narration, source list and image records can be prepared in parallel.
